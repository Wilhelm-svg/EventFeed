It used to be that the only way lodges could connect with travelers was through agents, DMCs and other middlemen. They played a vital role, opening doors to markets that felt distant and difficult to reach.

But the internet changed all that. Travelers now begin their journeys online, researching, comparing, and increasingly turning to AI recommendations.

The internet promised lodges a way to reach those guests directly — a chance to tell their story on their terms. For most lodges, this never happened. The internet's promise was buried under the weight of old habits and new complexities.

But that promise is still within reach...
Owning the first act

The best lodges have always treated hospitality like theatre. Every detail considered. Every touchpoint choreographed.

But the show doesn't start at check-in. It begins months earlier, when travelers start planning their dream safari. If you don't play a role in that act, someone else will write your part for you.

Guests meet your brand too late. At best, when they check in. At worst, never at all.
Lead role or line item

Claiming your place in the guest journey doesn't mean doing everything alone. It doesn't mean rejecting agents or spurning others who can amplify your message.

It means stepping into the lead role of your story, instead of accepting a supporting role in someone else's.

Building a connection to guests is how lodges craft their identity, protect their margins, and ensure they remain more than a line item in an itinerary.

Lodges who own their story will earn the loyalty that turns first-time visitors into lifelong advocates.
Step into the spotlight

Your lodge already knows how to hold a guest's attention once they arrive. The stage is set, the details are rehearsed, and the performance of hospitality is delivered with care.

But today the opening act is online. To stand out you need a direct presence where guests experience your voice long before they ever step into camp.

The lodges that thrive will be those that take their place, centre stage and tell their story.

What we brought back

A rate card can tell us what the day costs. It cannot tell us that Matt will turn a short fynbos walk into the best coffee martini of my life, or that a guide will spot an octopus all of us would have swum past. That is the knowledge we went to collect. For most of the team, it was also a refreshing way to experience the city they live in. As Pretoria residents of who have spent a lot of time in Cape Town over our lives, Chris and I can attest to that fact. The entire dynamic of how well Escape and Explore looked after us is well worth the day, even from a local’s perspective.
